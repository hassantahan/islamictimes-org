Methodology

How the timetable is calculated

Prayer times use the Sun’s position for the selected place and date. Presets specify twilight angles for Fajr, Maghrib, or ʿIshāʾ, or a fixed interval where the method calls for one. Settings offers custom angles and a midnight convention. The timetable flags high-latitude estimates when a solar event is unavailable. PrayTimes calculation guide

The site’s Jaʿfarī preset uses the Leva parameters, including 16° for Fajr. The Najaf office publishes Fajr times based on 18°. I.M.A.M. guidance on Fajr

ʿAṣr conventions

Jaʿfarī and Tehran

On Prayer Times, these presets and Jaʿfarī-based custom angles show Sayyid Sistani’s preferred ʿAṣr window. Under his ruling, you may pray ʿAṣr as soon as you finish Ẓuhr. Sayyid Sistani · ruling 717

Standard

ʿAṣr starts when an upright object’s shadow grows by one object height beyond its noon length. This is the Mālikī, Shāfiʿī, and Ḥanbalī rule, also held by some Ḥanafī jurists. One-shadow ruling · Tirmidhī 149

Ḥanafī

The option follows Abū Ḥanīfa’s well-known ruling. ʿAṣr starts when the shadow grows by twice the object’s height beyond its noon length. Al-Marghīnānī · al-Hidāyah, Prayer Times

With Method default, a Jaʿfarī or Tehran calendar schedules ʿAṣr at the opening of the preferred window. Prayer Calendar applies any selected convention and mosque offsets to the timetable and downloads.
