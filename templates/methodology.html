Reading the Sun

Altitude and depression
Altitude measures the angle from the observer’s horizontal line of sight to the Sun’s centre. It is positive above the horizon and negative below it. Depression is the size of the angle below the horizon.
Sunrise and sunset
The upper edge of the Sun appears at the horizon when its centre is about 0.83° below it. That standard angle allows for the Sun’s apparent radius and typical atmospheric refraction. Terrain and local weather can shift the visible event.
Solar transit and legal noon
The Sun crosses the local meridian at solar transit, which supplies the main Ẓuhr entry. The faḍīlah calculation takes legal noon as the midpoint between sunrise and sunset. These times usually fall close together.

The five prayers

Fajr
True dawn is the light spreading across the eastern horizon. The timetable estimates its onset from the Sun’s morning depression angle. The morning prayer period ends at sunrise.
Ẓuhr
The main timetable uses solar transit as the midday marker. The selected convention determines how the afternoon is shown.
ʿAṣr
Standard and Ḥanafī methods use afternoon shadow length. The Shia presets show the faḍīlah window within the shared afternoon period.
Maghrib
Sunni presets use calculated sunset or add one minute, as shown in the table below. The Shia presets use a depression angle to estimate when the eastern redness passes overhead.
ʿIshāʾ
Most presets use an evening depression angle to estimate when twilight fades. Umm al-Qura adds a fixed interval to Maghrib. In the shared Shia evening period, ʿIshāʾ may be offered after Maghrib.

ʿAṣr and shadow length

As the Sun descends, the shadow of an upright object lengthens. To measure the growth, subtract the length the shadow already had at noon. The library converts each target shadow length to a solar altitude and computes when the Sun reaches it.

Standard · one object height
The usual Mālikī, Shāfiʿī and Ḥanbalī threshold, also held by Abū Yūsuf and Muḥammad al-Shaybānī.
Ḥanafī · two object heights
The threshold attributed to Abū Ḥanīfa in al-Hidāyah.
Jaʿfarī · after Ẓuhr

Faḍīlah

For the Shia methods, the timetable shows faḍīlah, the preferred time within a prayer’s valid period. The Ẓuhr and ʿAṣr windows follow Sayyid Sistani’s rulings for ordinary conditions. Each fraction measures shadow growth beyond its legal-noon length, as a share of the object’s height.

ẒuhrLegal noon to 4/72/7

The library checks the afternoon Sun’s geometric altitude to find each shadow threshold. It needs local sunrise and sunset to estimate the window. Sistani gives separate guidance for intense heat.

Dawn

Fajr’s preferred time opens at true dawn and ends when dawn light reaches the whole horizon, apparently before redness appears in the east. Sunrise closes the valid prayer period.

The shaded estimate ends at civil dawn, when the Sun reaches 6° below the horizon. This is a rough proxy for the whole horizon brightening. Changing the Fajr angle moves only the opening. A locally measured angle in Settings can replace the closing estimate.

Evening twilight

For a non-traveller, western redness disappearing marks the end of Maghrib’s preferred time and the start of ʿIshāʾ’s. The latter ends one-third of the way from sunset to the next dawn. For travellers, Maghrib’s preferred time extends through the first quarter of the night.

The ≈ marks an estimate. The default uses latitude and season, up to 55° north or south. Across four held-out sites, its mean absolute error was 8 minutes, compared with 14 minutes for a fixed 14° crossing, weighting each site equally. The fixed angle was closer in Tucson and near Ankara.

Experimental estimates

In Settings, the blend moves 37% from the seasonal time toward the 14° crossing. It uses the seasonal time when the crossing is absent. Solar descent uses the Sun’s drop in the first half-hour after sunset.

For Shia presets, the ʿIshāʾ entry shows this preferred window when available. An explicit custom ʿIshāʾ angle shows its calculated time alongside the estimate.

High latitudes

When a required event is missing at high latitude, the site can substitute an estimate. At polar latitudes the library can use sunrise and sunset from a reference latitude. Twilight estimates count back from sunrise for Fajr or forward from sunset for Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ. The angle-based rule uses the twilight angle divided by 60 as the fraction of the night. Kuwait and Qatar use a half-night rule. Fixed-interval ʿIshāʾ follows the adjusted Maghrib.

The timetable flags affected dates and uses a dash for unavailable times. Faḍīlah windows require the underlying local solar events, so a preferred range may be absent even when estimated prayer times are available.

Dates and the local clock

Location and daylight saving

The selected coordinates determine the Sun’s path and the IANA time zone. The timetable uses the date, clock and UTC offset at that location, including daylight saving where applicable. A next-day marker means the event falls after civil midnight.

IANA · time zone database

Islamic midnight

The library takes the elapsed midpoint from sunset to the following Fajr or sunrise, as specified by the preset or midnight setting. With a sunset at 18:00 and Fajr at 05:00, it falls at 23:30 if the clock offset stays constant. On a clock-change night the midpoint is calculated on the UTC timeline and then converted back to local time.

Status on Prayer Times

With the Shia preset defaults, the live display highlights both prayers during the shared afternoon and evening periods. The timeline uses a deeper shade where the afternoon faḍīlah windows overlap. The display shows “Between prayers” during breaks in the timetable.

Hijri dates

The displayed Hijri date is calculated and may differ from local crescent observance.
